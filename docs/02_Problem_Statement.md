# Problem Statement

YogaVerse AI is being built around a real pilot business, Yoga By Rachna Shah. The initial product thesis is that the business has strong wellness expertise and content, but growth is constrained when discovery, personalization, instructor operations, and follow-up depend heavily on manual effort.

Each problem below is intentionally framed as a hypothesis to validate rather than as a proven fact.

## Student problems

### HYP-S01 — Discovery is too broad
**Hypothesis:** Yoga beginners struggle to choose routines that fit their goals, experience, schedule, and stated wellness needs.
**Validation:** Interview 10+ students and review their first-session discovery behavior.
**Success criterion:** 7+ participants describe choice overload or uncertainty as a meaningful barrier.
**Kill criterion:** Fewer than 5 participants report a meaningful discovery problem.

### HYP-S02 — Personalization affects consistency
**Hypothesis:** Users are more likely to continue a practice when the starting plan feels personally relevant.
**Validation:** Compare intent to continue between users receiving a personalized recommendation and users browsing generic content.
**Success criterion:** Personalized group shows at least 1.5x higher stated intent to continue in the pilot.
**Kill criterion:** Difference remains below 1.2x after 100 comparable users.

### HYP-S03 — Instructor selection is difficult
**Hypothesis:** Users cannot confidently choose an instructor from a basic profile without clearer matching signals.
**Validation:** User testing on a static instructor directory versus a recommendation flow.
**Success criterion:** 60%+ of test users can identify a preferred instructor and explain why.
**Kill criterion:** No measurable improvement in decision confidence.

### HYP-S04 — Generic AI lacks brand trust
**Hypothesis:** Users are more likely to trust wellness guidance when it is grounded in the methodology and content of a known instructor/business.
**Validation:** Compare reactions to generic AI answers with answers grounded in approved Yoga By Rachna Shah content.
**Success criterion:** Brand-grounded responses score at least 0.5 points higher on a 5-point trust rating.
**Kill criterion:** No meaningful trust difference.

## Instructor problems

### HYP-I01 — Repetitive operations consume teaching time
**Hypothesis:** Independent instructors spend 10+ hours per week on repetitive administration, follow-ups, scheduling, and content tasks.
**Validation:** Time-use interviews with 5+ instructors and a one-week activity log for at least 2 instructors.
**Success criterion:** Median self-reported repetitive workload exceeds 8 hours/week, with at least 3 instructors above 10 hours.
**Kill criterion:** Median workload is below 5 hours/week.

### HYP-I02 — Digital presence is a bottleneck
**Hypothesis:** Qualified instructors often lack the time or skills to create a strong profile and consistent marketing content.
**Validation:** Audit current online presence of 10 instructors and interview 5 about content creation.
**Success criterion:** 6+ report profile/content creation as a recurring burden.
**Kill criterion:** Fewer than 4 report this as a meaningful issue.

### HYP-I03 — Instructors lack useful business visibility
**Hypothesis:** Independent instructors do not have a simple view of leads, conversion, attendance, and retention.
**Validation:** Ask instructors to show current tracking methods and the questions they cannot answer today.
**Success criterion:** 4+ of 5 cannot answer at least two core business questions without manual effort.
**Kill criterion:** Most instructors already have reliable lightweight analytics.

## Business problems

### HYP-B01 — Founder capacity limits growth
**Hypothesis:** Growth of Yoga By Rachna Shah is constrained by the amount of teaching, onboarding, follow-up, and decision-making that currently depends on Rachna personally.
**Validation:** Map one week of founder activities and identify work that can be delegated, automated, or standardized.
**Success criterion:** At least 20% of identified weekly workload is potentially automatable or delegable without reducing quality.
**Kill criterion:** Less than 10% of workload is a credible automation opportunity.

### HYP-B02 — Instructor onboarding can be shortened
**Hypothesis:** A structured AI-assisted onboarding flow can reduce instructor onboarding from weeks of fragmented work to a few working days.
**Validation:** Time the current onboarding process for 2–3 instructors, then prototype the AI-assisted workflow.
**Success criterion:** 50%+ reduction in elapsed onboarding effort while retaining admin approval and quality checks.
**Kill criterion:** Less than 25% reduction or unacceptable quality loss.

### HYP-B03 — Recurring revenue is an opportunity
**Hypothesis:** A subscription layer around personalized programs, content access, and ongoing guidance can create more predictable revenue than one-off sessions alone.
**Validation:** Test willingness to pay with 20+ existing or prospective users using three simple plan concepts.
**Success criterion:** 20%+ indicate credible willingness to pay for a recurring plan at a tested price point.
**Kill criterion:** Fewer than 10% show willingness to pay or users strongly prefer one-off purchase only.

## Product implication

Phase 1 should prove one narrow loop: **discover → assess → personalize → recommend → convert**. Instructor onboarding and subscription mechanics will be designed into the platform architecture but should only expand after the student-facing loop is validated.
